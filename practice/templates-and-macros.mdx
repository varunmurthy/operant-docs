Templates and macros are the foundation of how OrthOS writes your clinical reports. A template is the overall structure of a report, such as a new patient consult or an operative report. A macro is a reusable block of text, such as a procedure narration or a physical exam description, that OrthOS inserts into a report when the transcript mentions the right trigger phrases.

You build and edit templates and macros on the web. The iPad and iPhone apps let you browse them but not edit them.

## What templates are

A template defines the sections of a report and how they should be arranged. For example, a new patient consult template might include Chief Complaint, History of Present Illness, Review of Systems, Past Medical History, Physical Exam, Assessment, and Plan. An operative report template includes Preoperative Diagnosis, Procedure, Anesthesia, Findings, and Postoperative Plan.

Each template can also have AI instructions: rules that tell OrthOS how to fill the template. For example, you can add a rule that says "If a tourniquet was used, add the tourniquet sentence to the pre-op paragraph." These instructions guide report generation but never appear in the final report itself.

## What macros are

A macro is a prewritten block of text that OrthOS inserts word for word when it recognizes the right procedure or topic in the transcript. For example, if you have a macro for ACL reconstruction with BTB autograft, OrthOS inserts your exact narration whenever the transcript mentions that procedure.

Each macro has:

- A name
- A category, such as Operative Narrations, Proposed Procedures, Physical Exams, or Post-Op
- A body region, such as Knee, Shoulder, or Foot & Ankle
- Trigger phrases that help OrthOS match the macro to what was said
- The macro content itself

## Create or edit a template

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the template library">
    On the web, go to **Templates** in the main navigation. You will see templates grouped by folder, such as New Patient Consult, Operative Report, and Follow-Up Visit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a new template">
    Click **New Template** to create a blank template. It will be placed in the folder you currently have open.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Name the template and choose the encounter type">
    Give the template a clear name and select the encounter type it belongs to, such as New Patient, Operative Report, or Follow-Up Visit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Edit the content">
    Use the editor to write the report skeleton. You can type text, add headings, and insert placeholders for fields that OrthOS will fill from the transcript.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add AI instructions (optional)">
    Switch to the **Report-wide rules** tab to add rules that guide how OrthOS fills the template. These are separate from the report text itself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**. The template is available immediately for the next report.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Create or edit a macro

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the macro library">
    In the Templates page, switch to the **Macros** tab. Macros are grouped by category.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a new macro">
    Click **New Macro** to create a blank macro.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Fill in the details">
    Enter the macro name, choose or type a category, select a body region if applicable, and add trigger phrases separated by commas. Trigger phrases are the words OrthOS listens for in the transcript to decide when to use this macro.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write the macro content">
    Type the full text that OrthOS should insert. This text is used verbatim. It is your own wording, not rewritten by OrthOS.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Click **Save**.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Archive a template or macro

If you no longer want a template or macro to appear in pickers, you can archive it. Archived items are hidden from new reports but existing reports that used them keep their content.

To archive, open the template or macro and click the delete icon. Confirm the action. The item will be deactivated.

## How templates and macros are used in reports

When OrthOS processes a visit, it does the following:

1. Classifies the encounter type, such as new patient consult or operative report.
2. Selects the matching template.
3. Identifies the body region and specific procedure from the transcript.
4. Matches and inserts the correct macros.
5. Fills any placeholders from what was said during the visit.
6. Follows the AI instructions you added to the template.

If the transcript does not mention a topic, that section is left blank. OrthOS does not invent information.

## Mobile access

On iPad and iPhone, you can browse templates and macros to review what is available. You cannot create, edit, or archive them from mobile. If you need to make changes, use the web app.

<Tip>
  Start with a small set of templates and macros for your most common visit types. You can always add more as your practice grows.
</Tip>
